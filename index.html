About

I work on questions at the intersection of international trade, firm behaviour, and industrial change. My research is motivated by how firms adapt when their economic environment changes: what they choose to produce, how they organise their activities, and where they direct innovative effort. I approach these questions using empirical analysis and economic modelling.

Current Interests

My current research studies different forms of firm adjustment to changes in their economic environment. One part looks at how access to global markets affects the behaviour of multiproduct firms, especially their incentives to integrate different products into broader systems. Another part looks at how offshoring and access to lower production costs affect firms’ innovation choices, and in particular whether this shifts them away from more novel and exploratory technologies. A third project studies how corporate taxation interacts with firm ownership, examining whether firms with different types of owners respond differently to tax policy and whether tax changes affect ownership structures themselves. More broadly, I am interested in how changes in international integration, production costs, and policy environments shape firm behaviour and industrial change.
